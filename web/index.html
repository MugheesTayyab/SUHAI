|1⟩The problem

40 kWh of sun.
Four services that want it.

Sun peaks at midday and is gone after dark. The battery holds one 5 kWh block. Someone has to decide which service runs when, and some of them will have to wait.

Numbers are the benefit of running a service in that slot. All values are synthetic. The clinic's vaccine fridge is powered before planning starts and never competes.

|3⟩The quantum method

Eleven qubits where the standard method needs twenty.

The usual way adds helper qubits that only count spare energy. Suhai instead fines each of the 16 smallest rule breaking combinations, so every qubit is a real decision.

|5⟩Limits

What these results do not show.

No speedup
Checking all 2,048 days takes well under a second on a laptop. QAOA is slower here.
Synthetic data
Energy figures and benefit scores are the team's own judgment, written down so anyone can question them.
Ideal simulator
Every run is noise free. Real hardware would lower the share of valid answers.
The best day is rare
It appears in 1 to 7 percent of answers. We keep the best valid one out of 4,096.
Not a pure QUBO
Two fines tie three decisions together, which adds two higher order terms.
Growth
The fine list stays short because each slot has few services. A crowded slot would grow it quickly.
