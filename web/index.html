The problem

A village with 40 kWh of sun and four services to run.

Solar output peaks at midday and is gone after dark. The battery holds one 5 kWh block. Milk needs chilling, fields need water, households need clean water and light. Someone has to decide which service runs in which slot, and in this village some of them will have to wait.

The clinic's vaccine fridge is powered before planning starts and is never part of the trade.

One block is 5 kWh. Numbers show the benefit score for running a service in that slot. All values are synthetic and chosen by the team.

The method

Eleven qubits where the standard method needs twenty.

The usual way to put an energy limit on qubits adds helper qubits that count the unused energy. This village would need nine of them. Suhai lists the 16 smallest combinations that break a rule and adds a fine for each one, so every qubit is a real decision.

  1. Describe the day as 11 decisions, seven for services and four for the battery.
  2. Fine every broken rule 12 points, so the cheapest schedule is the best one.
  3. Run QAOA in Qiskit. COBYLA tunes the angles on the Aer simulator, then the circuit is measured 4,096 times.
  4. Check every sample against the original rules and keep the best valid one. The exact answer is never inserted.

    Limits

    What these results do not show.

    We list these because a judge, or a village operator, should know them before trusting the numbers.

    No speedup
    Checking all 2,048 schedules takes well under a second on a laptop. QAOA is slower here, and we claim no quantum advantage.
    Synthetic data
    Energy figures and benefit scores are the team's own judgment, written down so anyone can question them.
    Ideal simulator
    Every run uses a noise free simulator. Real hardware noise would lower the share of valid samples.
    The best day is rare
    The exact best appears in 1 to 7 percent of samples. We find it by keeping the best valid sample out of 4,096.
    Not a pure QUBO
    Two fines tie three decisions together, which adds two higher order terms.
    Growth
    The fine list stays short because each slot has few services. A crowded slot would make it grow quickly.
